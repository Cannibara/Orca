Methodology

How Orca estimates fund results

Everything comes from public 13F filings plus daily stock prices. 13Fs only show what a fund held on the last day of each quarter, so trade prices, P&L and returns are estimates.

What a 13F covers

  • Long positions in US-listed stocks, ETFs and some options, as of quarter end.
  • No short positions, cash, bonds, non-US stocks or private holdings.
  • Filed up to 45 days after the quarter ends.

So a fund's real return can differ a lot from the return of its 13F book.

Trade prices

We can't see trade dates, only the change in shares between quarter ends. All of a quarter's buying or selling is assumed to happen at that quarter's volume-weighted average price:

typical price = (high + low + close) ÷ 3 quarter VWAP = Σ(typical price × volume) ÷ Σ volume

The quarter's low and high are shown next to every estimate.

P&L per stock

buy: cost += shares bought × VWAP sell: realised += shares sold × (VWAP − avg cost) unrealised = shares held × latest price − cost total = realised + unrealised + dividends

Fund returns

quarter return = (end value − start value − net buying + dividends) ÷ (start value + ½ × net buying) year = the four quarters chained

What if they'd kept it

For every sale we track the stock's return from the estimated sale price to today and over the next 12 months, against SPY. "IRR if held" keeps the same buys but values every share bought at today's price.
